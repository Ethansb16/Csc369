# How I Addressed Feedback:

The feedback said that the paper lacked background and explanation, needed visualizations, made too strong a claim that the patterns were truley exploitable. To address this, I added a Background section explaining mean reversion and why crypto markets are prone to it, while also introducing the execution cost factors that complicate execution which effect profitability. I softened the hypothesis from claiming the patterns are "exploitable" to stating they are "statistically significant" and reframed exploitability as a secondary question to be tested rather than assumed. I extended the analysis to model three layers of execution cost beyond exchange fees: order timing lag (entering at the next candle's open instead of the dip candle's close), spread estimation using each candle's high-low range as a proxy, and slippage modeled as a fraction of the entry candle's volatility. Since the data was just OHLVC, I had to estimate the neccesary data which leads to less accurate results. The results are presented in a new cost degradation model that shows returns turning negative for all 20 pairs once these costs are included, which is discussed in a new Execution Cost Impact section. The conclusion was revised to reflect that while the mean-reversion pattern is statistically real and survives basic fee deductions, it does not survive realistic execution complexities.
